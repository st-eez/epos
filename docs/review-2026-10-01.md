## First fixes

| Priority | Defect and evidence | Change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P1 | Native NSTextView reproduction showed that preview over selected `world` followed by cancellation changed `hello world` to `hello ` | Skip preview for selected text; companion validates selections and live composition ownership, pins its concrete client, refuses ambiguous client sets, and blocks either final backend after losing live composition ownership |
| P1 | After a socket timeout, a late mark acknowledgement could be mistaken for cancellation, hiding an unsafe cleanup reply | Close unaligned connections, reject malformed framing, and refuse final delivery when required cleanup is unconfirmed |
| P1 | Releasing while analyzer startup was suspended bypassed captured audio and reported cancellation | Stop the mic at release, hand startup audio to the analyzer, then finalize and deliver once |
| P1 | `start()` awaited framework startup without a bound, even when finish timed out | Bound the caller's startup wait and force-close a timed-out session; regression uses a framework stand-in that ignores cancellation |

| P2 | An old socket thread could execute queued commands against a newer preview pass | Validate current connection ownership immediately before command dispatch and preserve pass ownership through cleanup |
| P2 | `.failed` could leave the result stream open and delay the recognition-loss notice indefinitely | Treat failure as terminal immediately, stop capture, show the notice while held, and preserve recognized text until release |
| P2 | Capture setup or device recovery could fail after enabling voice processing, then skip cleanup because capture was not running | Use the same input-path unwind on successful stop and failed open or recovery |

## Follow-up fixes and recording ownership

The two remaining P2 defects and the ownership refactor were implemented in
three isolated worktrees, then integrated and independently reviewed.

- **Correction evidence:** each recording freezes one canonicalizer. The same
  matching pass returns canonical text and applied rule IDs for output and
  evidence. Editing, deleting, or adding rules during a hold affects the next
  recording. The regression covers both recordings and persisted provenance.
- **Ambiguous IME delivery:** a fully sent commit whose reply is lost displays
  **Check the field** through teardown. It makes no second write. The regression
  inserts through the IME stand-in before losing the acknowledgement and checks
  the notice, one insertion, and no keystroke fallback.
- **Recording ownership:** [RecordingSession](../Sources/Epos/App/RecordingSession.swift)
  owns capture, startup audio, recognition, the correction snapshot, insertion,
  preview, release, and cleanup. AppCoordinator retains readiness, key state,
  and presentation. Cleanup finishes before another hold can start. Late
  callbacks from a completed hold cannot change the next one, and the old
  audio relay stops forwarding while preserving queued startup audio.

The independent simplification pass removed retired coordinator transform
helpers and an evidence-store lookup. Final/display parity now traverses a
complete recording instead of comparing production with a test-only helper.
The shared correction matcher replaces separate provenance compilation.

The companion remains production code inside `probes/`. Its string command and
reply contracts are maintained separately from the app's session state. Keep
ownership and acknowledgement semantics explicit before changing packaging or
introducing a shared protocol.

## Test quality

An independent test audit checked lifecycle, preview, routing, socket,
transcriber, diagnostic, and sampled correction/evaluation coverage. It found
and corrected concrete weaknesses:

- Settings tests use unique defaults suites; socket tests await actual peer
  closure and command consumption instead of racing the server.
- Bootstrap fixtures use fake target observers and disable the live companion.
  Successful recordings await teardown.
- The no-input finish deadline uses a bounded expectation. Its old structured
  task group could wait forever for a cancellation-ignoring child.
- Audio buffering tests assert retained frame identities and order, catching
  loss of the beginning of speech even when buffer and drop counts match.
- Direct XCTest runs now suppress real bells and windows even without runner
  environment markers. The existing policy regression failed before the fix and
  passed afterward under that exact runner, without invoking a recording.
- The pre-start accessibility wake test observes the forbidden work directly;
  its queue fixture has a bounded startup wait.
- Controlled gates replace correction-sampling and notice-expiry sleeps. The
  canceled sampling fixture now proves it accepted an insertion before canceling.

Mutation checks made the relevant regressions fail when removing stale-audio
forwarding protection, frozen correction evidence, edit-capture cancellation,
ambiguous-delivery feedback, or notice-generation checks. This establishes
sensitivity for those fixes. Repetition reduces uncertainty about scheduling.
The current bundle passed ten runs of 43 selected regressions in two concurrent
processes, with zero failures. Those runs exposed missing cue suppression in the
runner policy, which was corrected afterward. No audit or finite repeat run
proves the entire suite free of flakes.

## Evidence limits and verification

The historical diagnostic logs inspected during the initial audit ended
August 8, 2026. `scripts/audit` found 407
schema-1 recording starts, including 10 target refusals, six delivery mismatches,
and one incomplete session. These are dated leads, not current defect counts.
The 175 empty transcripts and 28 no-input sessions cannot be labeled lost speech
without inspecting their capture context.

The default saved accuracy artifact contained 114 rows and 106 exact outputs,
but failed its authoritative corpus join. The audit reported
`authoritative_join_mismatch` and `verifiedAccuracy=false`. Those rows cannot
support a current accuracy claim. Select and verify a current signed artifact
and confirmed reference corpus explicitly.

Verification passed the strict Swift build and final Swift suite with 371
tests, three opt-in evaluation skips, and zero failures. SwiftLint without a
cache, ten native companion safety checks, and the audit self-test passed.
The initial audit also passed deterministic corpus, confirmation, labeling,
and migration checks. Opt-in evaluation skips remain separate from passes.

The signed app and companion were rebuilt and installed. The installed main
binary matches the build hash and passes strict signature verification. The
companion is registered, enabled, and selectable. Installed-app startup logs
show permissions requests completed, the speech asset reserved, and bootstrap
completed with a 16 kHz capture format. The final app was relaunched normally
with the developer trigger disabled. Startup does not establish physical fn-key
or field-delivery behavior.

Native UI inspection timed out, so field delivery and daily-app acceptance
remain unverified. Diagnostic rows produced by direct test-runner experiments
are synthetic and must be excluded from real recording/accuracy counts.

The replacement gate remains the daily-app matrix in
[development.md](development.md#runtime-acceptance), including short and long
holds, selection replacement, focus movement, 50 consecutive recordings,
permissions, microphone changes, and measured latency, startup, and memory.
Keep recognition accuracy and backend acceptance separate from these checks.
